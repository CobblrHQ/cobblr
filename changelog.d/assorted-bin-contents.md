**A bin of fifty jumbled things can say so.** Instead of pretending you will one day type in every adapter in the box, record it as "assorted adapters, roughly 50", and break it into kinds later only if you want to. Anything you do count keeps its identity, including its printed label.

## docs

**Not everything is worth counting.** A bin of assorted adapters, a jar of odd screws, a drawer of cables: you are never going to enter them one by one, and Cobblr should not pretend otherwise. Record the whole lot as one thing instead.

Give it a name and roughly how many, like "Assorted adapters, about 50". That is enough to find them again, and the bin stops looking empty just because you did not enumerate it.

**Estimates look like estimates.** Anything recorded as "roughly" is drawn softly, so at a glance you can tell what you have counted from what you have guessed at. In your item list it reads as "~50" rather than a bare number, there are no plus and minus buttons to nudge it (a guess is not a thing you increment by one), and it is never flagged as low stock for being short on a count nobody took. Cobblr also remembers when you made the guess.

**Break it down whenever you like, or never.** An assortment can hold kinds: "DB25 gender changers, about 7", "serial adapters, about 6". Each is still an estimate, and they live inside the assortment rather than scattered loose in the bin.

**Counting something does not start it over.** When one kind turns out to matter, give it a real quantity and it becomes a counted item in place. It keeps everything it already had: its history, its tags, its photos, and the QR label already stuck on the thing. Scanning a newly bought one in files it alongside, and your estimate is left alone rather than being silently corrected.
