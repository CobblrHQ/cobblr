You can add tracking to an order yourself: paste the number or the carrier's tracking link, and tick the lines that are in that box. Each line, and the thing it becomes, now says where it is ("On its way: Amazon Logistics TBA…, expected Oct 11") or "Not shipped yet". An online order with no delivery date says "On its way, no date yet" and is asked about once, a week later.

## docs

**Adding tracking yourself.** On an order that has not arrived, choose **+ Add tracking** above its lines. Paste the tracking number, or the whole "Track your package" link from the store's email: Cobblr reads the number out of it. Tick the lines that are in this box. The lines no parcel carries yet start ticked; a line already in a parcel shows that parcel beside it and stays there unless you tick it. Leave them all ticked on an order's first box, and the number goes on the order itself. The scan inbox's tracking control and the order's **Tracking #** field take a link the same way, and so does Cobb ("add tracking TBA335321582286 to the Kingroon order, just the Multicolor").

**Where each thing is.** Every line of an order that ships in parcels says which one it is in: "On its way: Amazon Logistics TBA335321582286, expected Oct 11", or **Not shipped yet**. The thing a line becomes says the same on its own page, under **On its way**, until it arrives, and so does the receipt's row in the scan inbox (on a phone, under the row's **Details**).

**Online orders without a date.** An order confirmation, or a receipt that charged for shipping, is recorded as on its way rather than arrived, even before any tracking number. If the store gave no delivery date, the order says "On its way, no date yet": Cobblr never makes one up. A week after it was recorded it asks you once whether it has arrived. A real date, from the store or the carrier, replaces that.
