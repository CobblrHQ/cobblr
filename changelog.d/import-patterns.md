Yarn's Designs are now **Patterns**, and your pattern PDFs have a home. **Import patterns** takes a pile of PDFs or a zipped downloads folder: each PDF becomes a pattern with its file, its cover photo pulled out of the PDF, a zip's category folders fill in the category, and a PDF you already have is never added twice. A pattern also keeps its designer, where it came from and how hard it is, and it opens right in the app, on a phone too.

## docs

**Patterns (Yarn).** Turn on the Yarn bundle's **Patterns** table (it used to
be called Designs; updating renames it and keeps everything in it). Each
pattern owns its PDF, its photos and your notes, with Category, Designer,
Source and Difficulty, and the yarn and hooks it needs. Opening a pattern opens
its PDF in the app, with pinch to zoom and Keep screen on.

**Import patterns.** On the Patterns page, set **Source** for the batch if you
like (Ribblr, Ravelry, Etsy, …), then choose PDFs, a zipped folder, or both.
Each PDF becomes a pattern named after its file, with its cover photo. A zip
keeps its folders under **Patterns** in Files, and a top folder named like a
category (`Blankets/…`) fills in the category. Anything that is not a PDF stays
in Files without becoming a pattern. A PDF you already have is linked to its
pattern, never imported twice. The batch ends with one line, such as
*3 patterns made, 2 already here*.

**Keeping your Ribblr patterns.** Ribblr has no export and its terms forbid
automated downloading, so the rescue is by hand: download every pattern with a
download icon and the PDFs you uploaded yourself (a lapsed Ribblr+ locks those;
a month of Ribblr+ opens them again), ask designers of ePatterns for a PDF, and
optionally email Ribblr support for a copy of your data. Then zip the downloads
and use **Import patterns** with **Source: Ribblr**.
