Lay out a room like a puzzle: windows and doors that show their swing, closets, racks you can turn a quarter, the gap to the nearest wall while you drag, and an aisle check, on the floor plan and in Plan a layout.

## docs

**Doors, windows, closets and turning racks.** For fitting shelving into a room. In **Edit layout**, with nothing selected, the room's panel adds a **door**, a **window** or a **closet** on any of its walls (top, bottom, left or right). Select a wall to change what each opening is (door, window or plain opening), where it is and how wide. A door also says which way it opens (into the room or out, or left or right on an inside wall) and which side its hinge is on. A window has its sill height, 900 mm unless you change it. A window is drawn as a thin double line in the wall. A door is drawn with its leaf open and the quarter circle it sweeps, and that floor is kept clear: a rack standing in it turns the swing red, gets a red outline, and the list under the plan says "Rack 3 blocks the door in the bottom wall". A rack taller than a window's sill standing in front of it gets a softer note ("blocks the window"). Neither stops you putting it there. A **closet** is a recess against the wall with its own walls and a door on its front. It is an ordinary part of the room, so anything you put in it is filed in it, and its walls move with it when you drag it. Select a closet to change its door's width, hinge and which way it opens. To **turn a rack** a quarter, select it and press **Turn 90°**, press **R** while you drag it, or hold a finger still on it on a phone. Its width and depth swap on the plan and it stays inside the room. The thicker edge in Edit layout is the side you reach it from. A turned rack is still the same rack, so its shelves still say what hangs off its front. While you drag anything on a floor, the gap to the nearest wall or rack on each side is drawn with its size. **Plan a layout** draws the room's walls, doors, windows and closets too, turns racks the same way, and flags a rack in a door's swing in each option, so two options compared side by side show which one blocks the door. **Check aisles** (900 mm wide unless you type another width) lists every gap between two racks, or a rack and a wall, that is narrower than that. The printed layout draws the doors, windows and closets and lists what is in the way. Cobb reads the same flags when he plans a layout, and can move and turn racks in a draft. A floor plan file can carry all of it: a door's `swing` and `hinge`, a window's `sill_mm`, a zone's `closet` and a container's `rot`.
