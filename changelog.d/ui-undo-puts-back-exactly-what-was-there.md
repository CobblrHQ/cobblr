# Core concepts

## 2. Core concepts

A handful of words mean specific things in cobblr. Skim this once.

**Tenant / org / workspace**: same thing, three names. Your workspace. Backed by its own Postgres DB. Other tenants can't see your rows. One login can own / be a member of many workspaces; use the header chip to switch between them. Multi-tenant is not retrofittable; it's table stakes.

**Module** (also "connector"): A pluggable feature pack. Each one owns its own tables, REST surface, and UI. Modules don't import each other's code. They talk through the platform. Cobblr ships ten modules today; users can disable any one and the rest keep working.

**Bands**: Every module belongs to one of four bands. **Kernel** (not a band, actually: the tiny set of primitives the platform itself is built from; no module fits here). **Foundational**: the platform can't work without it (very small set; no disable toggle). **Stock**: ships with Cobblr by default, toggleable per workspace (the Apple-first-party-apps band: gold, deletable). **Marketplace**: community-authored, downloadable, install-to-enable (future). **User**: custom-built for one specific app. Stock modules come in two flavours: **capabilities** (views, search, scan, AI, apps, labels-qr, …, ambient plumbing, *auto-enabled* in every workspace because there's no decision to make) and **domains** (inventory, projects, machines, assets, purchases, labels, digifab, the nouns you choose to manage, *opt-in*). See [`docs/architecture/module-layers.md`](../architecture/module-layers.md) → "Capabilities vs domains" for the rationale.

**Traits**: Six axes (Tangibility, Identity, Containment, Time, Lifecycle, Persistence), twelve traits total, each entity kind declares where it sits. Cross-module actions match by trait predicate: `labels:print` declares `appliesTo: { traits: ["physical"] }` so it shows up on any physical entity (parts, machines, assets) without naming any module by ID. Lets the action set grow open-endedly as new modules ship. See [`docs/architecture/traits.md`](../architecture/traits.md).

**Entity**: A thing a module manages. *Parts* are entities owned by inventory; *projects* and *tasks* by projects; *labels* by labels. Each entity belongs to a **kind**, written as `<module>:<thing>`: e.g. `inventory:part`, `projects:task`. Each kind declares its traits + an `exposableFields` whitelist (which fields are readable by other modules, read-time trust boundary).

**Action**: A button-like operation a module exposes that *can be aimed at an entity*. Examples: `labels:print` (queue a label), `projects:set-dep-satisfied` (flip task deps). Actions declare what kinds they apply to via an `appliesTo` predicate: `{ any: true }`, `{ kinds: [...] }`, `{ traits: [...] }`, or `{ hasFieldRole: "..." }` (combinable).

**Wire** (aka *binding*): A user-editable row that says **"when [trigger] fires, run action Y on [target], optionally with template T."** Wires are how cross-module behaviour gets configured *without anyone hand-importing anything*. Three triggers: `event` (named bus event), `user-invoked` (button click), `schedule` (RRULE-driven). **Target** defaults to `"self"` (action runs on the source) but can be `{ rel, dir?, kind? }` to opt into pairing traversal, *"when stock changes on a Part, fire the action on every Task that depends on it."* See [`docs/architecture/wires-and-bundles.md`](../architecture/wires-and-bundles.md).

**Bundle**: A publishable manifest that packs a set of wires + custom field defs under one identifier. You can install one with a click and uninstall just as cleanly. You can also export your own org as a bundle and re-import it on a fresh org. Beyond gluing modules together, bundles are *user-experience presets for a use case*: add Lego-collector custom fields + label templates and `inventory:part` looks and behaves like a Lego collection tool, without any code in inventory changing.

**App Builder** (`/build`, the **Build** wand in the header): Don't want to hand-write a bundle? Describe what you want in plain English ("add a *warranty expires* date to parts, and when one runs low print a reorder label") and the builder **compiles a prompt** with exactly the context the task needs (the relevant entity-kind schemas + the actions those kinds can be wired to + the output shape). Phase 1 is **copy-paste**: run that prompt in *any* AI (your own ChatGPT/Claude, zero cost to us), paste the manifest back, and Cobblr **validates it before anything changes**: the same gate as bundle install, so a valid result is guaranteed installable, and a broken one comes back with plain, fixable errors (and a one-click *repair prompt*) instead of corrupting your app. Preview → **Apply**. The promise: *Cobblr never applies a broken artifact.* (Hosted "build it for me" is a later phase.)

**Ask Cobb** (the assistant launcher in the header's right cluster): a chat side-panel where you can ask about your workspace or tell it to do something ("add a part called Widget", "what's low on stock?"); it always shows you the change and waits for your confirm before running it. **Cobb**, the little cobblestone-headed craftsman, is its face (and the face of the Build page). **Cobb reads your real data** when your AI supports tool calling (Anthropic, OpenAI, OpenRouter, tool-capable local servers, or a local AI that *runs tools itself*: set **"How this AI runs tools"** on its connection to the MCP option and Cobb reads your workspace through it, read-only): questions about your stock/projects/notes make him search and fetch your actual records, through your own permissions, before answering, and one request that asks for several changes ends in **one plan with one Confirm**: its steps in the order they run, each saying what it touches, worded after the steps before it (removing Yarn after deleting its only record never says Yarn keeps a record), and each saying whether it can be undone; Confirm runs them in order and stops at the first that cannot go through, saying what was done (besides creates and actions he can propose **field updates** and **deletes** where a module allows them). Asking again about the same thing replaces the earlier card: it folds to one line, "Replaced by the plan below", and can no longer be confirmed. His tools come from the same shared registry the MCP server exposes, so in-app Cobb and an external Claude can always do the same things. **You can point him at things.** The small Cobb button beside a record's other actions puts THAT record in context and opens the chat ready for your question, and **presses add up**: press Rack 5 then Rack 6 and Cobb has both, with the chip counting them ("2 locations"). Pressing a lit one drops just that one. Pointing at a record of a different kind starts again rather than mixing the two, since "these three" spanning racks and parts is not something Cobb could act on without guessing. It sits wherever the record does: on its own page, and on each row of a list that shows one (every area and container row on Locations, for instance). **Actions you bound to a kind ride the rows too.** Say once that printing a label applies to everything physical, and a **Print label** button appears on every one of those rows, not only on each record's own page. A module's actions that you have NOT bound stay on the record's page, where there is room to explain them; up to three of your own bound actions show per row. Anything that cannot be put right again asks before it runs (a row button sits between an edit pencil and a delete bin, so a print takes a second tap saying **sure?**). Hover it and the module says what it actually does, since a word like "print" does not say *when*: label printing queues the label, and the button says so with the count ("Queued, 3 in the queue"); if a walk-up printer is your default it prints there and then and the row says where ("Printed to …"), and if that printer cannot be reached from this browser a second line says what would make it print, never an error over the queue; a workspace with no printer set up sees the queue line only (press it again to drop it; it stays lit amber while it is the one in context). On a list, tick the rows you mean (nothing else to press: what is ticked is what Cobb is talking about, and **Ask Cobb** in the bar just opens him on it), or highlight text anywhere on the page, and a chip above the message box says what is in context ("12 locations", "11 inbox items", "Selected text"); "these" means exactly the ticked ones, and Cobb never asks which; your message carries those records themselves, so "delete duplicates" or "move these to the Garage" means the ones you picked rather than a search of the workspace. **A sent message keeps what it was sent with**: the chip appears beside it in the conversation, to the left of your words, so scrolling back to "tell me about these" still tells you which two racks you meant. The × drops it and sending clears it. A highlight is captured as you make it, so clicking into the box does not lose it, and it stays visibly highlighted on the page while you type about it. When the words name a record on that screen, the chip says the record and Cobb gets THAT, so highlighting a rack and asking about duplicates looks inside the rack rather than searching for racks by that name. The context stays for the whole conversation, so an answer to Cobb's follow-up still knows what you meant; the × drops it, clicking on empty space drops it, and starting a new conversation clears it (clicking into the message box does not, since that is you about to type about the thing). **He rearranges your navigation for you.** "Put Spices and Tea under Kitchen" is a change he proposes and you confirm, like any other: he matches the names to the sections you actually have, creates the heading if it does not exist, and moves them under it. Asking for something he cannot find gets a question rather than a guess, and "take Tea back out" undoes it. **A name two records share gets a question too, never a pick.** "I need a label on the CubePro" with a CubePro #9 and a CubePro #10 in the workspace does not print a label for whichever came first in the search: Cobb asks which one, and the two names appear as taps under his question; tapping one sends your own sentence back with that record named, and the label is proposed for that one. Your words decide: "CubePro 10", "the black CubePro", "the CubePro in the Garage" already say which, so no question is asked. Any fact Cobb read on the way counts, not only the name and the fields: where a thing is, once he has looked at what is in the Garage, picks the one that is there. It makes no difference whether he searched for the name or read through the whole list of machines; two records that answer to what you said get the question either way. When he does ask, each tap says what he read about that record ("CubePro #9 (in Garage)"), and he says only what he read: if he has not looked at where something is, he does not say where it is. Only changes ask this way (a label, an edit, a delete); a question about your things is answered from whatever matches. Up to six names are offered as taps; past that (twenty parts matching "bolt") Cobb names a few, says how many there are, and asks for a word that narrows it. **He changes your fields the same way.** "Remove Tea from the category choices, it is no longer a valid option" takes that one choice off the dropdown and leaves the rest; "add Aran to the yarn weight choices" adds one; a rename, a hide, a required flag are each one sentence. A change the workspace refused is said as a refusal, never drawn as a finished step. When several kinds of record have a field by that name, the one whose list offers the choice is the one meant, and the card says how many records still use it (they keep their value; it is just no longer offered). A refusal is said in your workspace's words, never with the tool's own field names, and every question ends with something to read: when Cobb has nothing to say, he says that rather than leaving a blank bubble. **What he knows about Cobblr itself is a list, not a memory.** Cobb is given the app's own features and where they live (navbar headings, instances, custom fields, wires, and so on), and asked anything outside that list he says he is not sure and points at Configuration rather than inventing a screen. He was previously told he knew how the app worked "because he is part of it", which is how a request for a navigation parent got answered with "create a new app" while the screen that does exactly that went unmentioned. **Cobb answers like a person, not like a database.** He leads with the answer, skips the internal shape of your records (a rack is "a rack", not "a container location configured as a top-level storage unit"), and never shows you an id: where he needs to refer to something he uses its name, and an id he cannot name is simply left out. That last part does not depend on the model behaving, it is stripped from every answer on the way to you. **A record he names is a chip you can open.** Wherever Cobb names one of your records, in a done card, a confirm card or his own answer, the name is a chip that opens the record, the words around it kept: ask which books you have and each book in the reply opens its own page. He only ever links a record he just looked up or changed, never a name he made up, and a name two records share stays plain text rather than a guess. **He reads a record the way its form shows it.** Each value he is given comes with the field's label and the explanation under it ("Replacement value: what it would cost to buy new today, not what you paid"), and a field with nothing in it is left out, so a price you never entered is said to be not recorded rather than filled in from another field. **A confirmation shows what it will write.** A card for a new record or an edit lists each value it will save, in the words the record's form uses ("Room: Bedroom", "Paid: 24"), and an edit shows what each field is now and what it will be. A move says where the thing is and where it goes ("Move Desk fan: Bedroom → Garage"). A value your words did not give him is marked **suggested by Cobb**, and the × beside it leaves it out of the change before you confirm. A link he writes himself is never taken on trust: if it points at a record, it is redrawn from that record's own address as the chip, or left as plain words when he did not look the record up, so a made-up address never reaches the screen. A word that only shares its start with a record (a shop named "Review test shop" beside a "Review imported toaster") is not that record. The chip works from the keyboard, tells a screen reader what kind of thing it opens ("The Hobbit, book"), and if the record has been deleted since the answer was written, pressing it says so instead of opening an error page. The green answer that appears while you type and the result of a command the workspace ran on its own name their records the same way. **The page keeps up:** when Cobb changes something, the lists behind the panel refresh as it happens rather than waiting for you to reload. **A half-written question survives a page refresh**, per workspace, on your device. **Cobb also knows what screen you're on:** each page tells him a one-line summary of what it's showing (e.g. the Scan Inbox's "19 pending, 16 waiting 2d+"; Purchases' open + in-transit orders; a Machines state breakdown; Inventory's low-on-stock count), so a question like "what do I have going on?" can reference what you're looking at, while a workspace-wide question still gets a workspace-wide answer. **A change is planned by your AI's stronger model.** When you ask Cobb to delete something, remove a feature, take a choice off a list or make a new list, the plan comes from the fuller model of the AI you chose for chat (Flash rather than Flash Lite on Google's free tier, Sonnet rather than Haiku on Claude), and a busy one never hands the plan to the lighter model; questions and everyday answers stay on the quicker one. Configuration → AI lists it under Chat as its own job, so you can pick a different AI for it. **Two consent chips at the top of the panel** control it per-person per-workspace, enforced server-side (off = the model never receives those tools): "Read my data", and a **three-way write mode** you cycle by clicking, **Changes: ask** (every write is a Confirm card, the default), **Changes: auto** (record creates and edits apply immediately and Cobb can chain them, and so do the actions that can be undone inside the workspace, like reordering locations or adjusting stock; a record **delete** still waits for your confirm even here, since a delete steered by text that lives in your workspace is not a risk worth taking automatically, and anything else that cannot be put right again, printing a label, commanding a device, emptying a list, still waits for your confirm and says so by name), **Changes: off**. **A stop never waits.** "Stop the laser", "stop that print" or "pause the resin printer" runs at once in any mode, and over MCP too: it can only stop or pause a machine, never start or resume one, so there is nothing to confirm. Starting and resuming still ask. This holds whichever way your AI is connected: a personal connection that makes the changes itself is recorded the same way, so its changes carry the same undo. **Undo puts it back, it does not do the opposite.** Each change records the record's whole state before and after, so a deleted record returns as the *same* record with the same id (children, labels and anything filed under it still point at it), and an edit is reverted in full rather than only in the fields the change named. **And it never takes back what YOU did.** If you edited the record after Cobb, only the parts still as he left them go back, and the panel names what it kept ("Left the notes as you set it"). If you changed the very field he changed, the undo declines rather than overruling you. Undoing a create will not delete a record you have since made yours, and undoing an edit will never resurrect a record you deleted. It also will not remove a place that still has things in it, since those would go with it: it says which ones and leaves it standing. **What it holds back, it offers**: the message names what was left and a button beside it takes those back too, so nothing is decided for you and nothing is a dead end. Taking that offer is a tracked change like any other, so it can itself be undone. A whole instruction goes back in one press. Auto is safe because of the **AI change ledger**: every write Cobb executes, confirmed or automatic, is recorded with a full before-image and shows an **↩ Undo** right in the chat (undo a create = deleted; undo an edit = old values back; undo a delete = recreated as a new record, said plainly; an undo is itself ledgered, so you can undo an undo). Parts, machines, assets, projects, tasks, and lists are fully creatable/editable/deletable through Cobb, and therefore undoable. **Ask for a SET and you get the set.** "Each rack should have Shelf 1 through 5" is one request that makes all of them, not sixty separate ones: it takes seconds instead of minutes, arrives as a single line in the chat ("Added 58 locations. 2 were already there"), and carries one **Undo** that puts every one of them back. Anything already there is counted rather than duplicated. Up to 200 records in one request. **Cobb can run workspace-level actions too**, the ones that act on a set rather than on one record (putting the locations under a parent in the order you want, renaming a label-code group). He proposes them with their arguments filled in and no record attached, and confirming runs them. **If your AI does not support tool calling** (a personal Claude bridge is the common case) he falls back to a simpler shape, and that shape can carry an action's arguments too. **A question interrupted by a restart ends too.** A question runs on the server, so if the server is restarted while Cobb is working on one, nobody is left to finish it. That now reads as unfinished the moment you look at it or reload, with a note that nothing was changed and to ask again, instead of a spinner that survives every refresh. **A question that goes unanswered now ends.** If your AI provider accepts the request and then goes quiet, the call is given up on after two minutes and the whole question after five, with a message saying so; nothing is changed. Before, the panel sat on "Thinking..." indefinitely. **The up and down arrows bring back what you sent**, the way a terminal does: press ↑ in the box for your last message, again for the one before, and ↓ to come forward. A half-typed message is kept while you look and comes back when you reach the end, and the list survives a refresh (it is per workspace, on your device, and clearing the conversation does not clear it). In a message that runs over several lines the arrows still move the cursor until it reaches the top or bottom line. **A long conversation keeps working:** Cobb is given the newest forty messages with each thing you send, however long the scrollback on your device has grown. **Your conversation survives a refresh, and so does the panel, and so does a question Cobb is still working on:** each workspace's chat is remembered on your device (only the plain text, not live Confirm cards), so reloading the page picks up where you left off; and because each question you send is a turn that runs on the server rather than inside your tab, reloading mid-answer, or opening the same workspace in a second tab, shows the same *working* state and then the same answer. **Some questions are answered before you send them.** The quickest are the ones about the screen you are on: "what page am I on?", "where am I", "what is on this page" are answered by the tab itself, instantly, with nothing asked of the server at all.  As you type one Cobb can answer from your own records, the answer appears in a green bubble in the conversation: counts ("how many locations do I have", and counts of a value inside your records: "how many Bambus?", "any deltas?", "which model do I have the most of?", counted across every field), where something is, what needs you, what is low. Green because it is a different kind of answer from the assistant's, and it says which of three places it came from: **from the page you are on** (the tab knew), **straight from your workspace** (one read of your records), or **from what Cobb already knows** (his built-in answers, the ones you can edit under Configuration, which cover "what can you do?", "how do I add a part", "where do I scan"). All three say **no AI used**, because none was. It works with no AI connected at all. Deliberately narrow, because an answer shown unprompted must never be a guess: an unfamiliar word, a noun matching two of your record types, or anything that would change something is left alone and goes to the assistant on enter, as before. **The answer appears as it is written.** A reply that takes ten seconds starts showing after about three, a few words at a time, under the list of what he has already done, so a long answer stops looking like a hang. It streams when the provider supports it and the turn is a plain answer rather than one calling tools, since a half-arrived tool call is not a thing to show. This holds however your AI is connected: when your assistant is a personal connection that runs the tools itself, each change it makes appears in the list as it happens rather than after, so a two-minute request reads as work instead of a spinner. While Cobb works, his speech bubble shows what he has DONE, a step at a time, rather than one word that keeps being replaced: "Reading your locations", "Searching for drill", "Running adjust stock". Each ticks green as it finishes, one that fails is marked rather than dropped, and the step in progress carries a running clock once the wait is long enough to wonder about. **The steps stay after the answer.** They fold under the reply into one line ("2 steps · 1 failed"); press it to see each step and how it went. When a step failed, the list is already open with the failed one's reason under it. **A step that did not happen is never said as done.** The answer is built from what the steps actually did: "Filing 11 items into Mugs did not happen: …", with the steps that did work shown as done cards above it, and a **Retry** card for the step that failed. **Making a list and filing into it works in either order.** "Create Mugs and file these 11 in it" makes the list first and then files all eleven, even when the AI tries the filing first. **A list Cobb names is a chip too**, like a record: press "Mugs" in the reply to open the list. A long turn reads as work with its reasons visible, instead of a hang. Reloading and if the panel was open when you reloaded it comes back **on that page**. It does not follow you around: open Cobb on the scan inbox, wander off to Inventory, and Inventory stays as you left it. Closing the tab forgets it, so a fresh visit never opens with a panel you left open yesterday; the **trash icon** in the panel header clears it. When you **open Cobb for a specific thing** (a screen's or a row's **Ask Cobb** button, a deep-link), the conversation stays exactly where it is: what you pointed him at shows as a chip above the message box, and nothing is inserted into the conversation itself. Earlier versions parked the previous turns behind an "Earlier" divider and scrolled to it, which moved the view out from under you for what was only a button press. History is kept on your device and, for your account only, on the server, so another tab or device opens on the same conversation. The **trash icon** clears it everywhere at once, and a reload straight after never brings it back. **Basic mode:** if this workspace has **no AI provider connected**, the panel says so *up front*, a strip at the top with a *Connect AI* link, before you type, instead of failing after your first message. In basic mode a message can be up to 2,000 characters; a longer one is not sent, the panel says how long it is, and the words stay in the box to shorten. In basic mode Cobb still answers the essentials with no AI at all (greetings, "what can you do", "how do I add a part", "where do I scan", "build my workspace"); those same answers are offered in a workspace that HAS AI too, worded for that world, so a connected workspace is never told that AI is not connected; and the empty panel, his greeting and "what can you do" name the built-in commands that make changes with no AI ("make Shelf 1 through 3 in Garage", "add a place called Blue bin in Shelf 1", "delete duplicates"), each run with **Do it** and put back with "undo". For open questions about your data, or a change no command covers, connect AI under **Configuration → AI**. (Connect a provider there, or route a personal key in via your account's **AI connections**.) **Some commands are there from the start.** Modules ship the sentences their own users commonly type, so **Configuration → Assistant** already lists a few under **Things this workspace can do on its own** before you have taught it anything, each labelled with the module it came from. Locations ships "make {label} {from} through {to} in {parent}", which numbers a run of shelves, bays or bins inside a place you name, and "add a place called {name} in {parent}". Cobblr itself ships "add {choice} to the {field} choices" and "remove {choice} from the {field} choices", so "add Aran to the yarn weight choices" is offered in those words the moment you type it, on any workspace, and runs the same field change the settings page would; name the field the way it appears on the form, and if two kinds of record carry a field with that name Cobb asks which rather than guessing. They work with no AI connected at all. A command your own workspace learned has an **export** that gives you the snippet to contribute it back, so something that turns out to be common can ship in the module instead of being taught again in every workspace; nothing is uploaded, the export is text for you to read and share if you choose. **Some jobs are worked out rather than filled in.** "Delete duplicates" cannot be a phrase with blanks, because what it does depends on what is there. Cobb looks for records with the same name in the same spot (places by what they are inside, parts by the bin they are filed in, and any other kind whose module has said how to read it) and says what he would remove and what he would keep ("Delete 2 duplicate places (Shelf 1, Shelf 2), keeping the original of each"), and waits for you to accept. No AI is involved, a selection scopes it to what you pointed at, and what it removes is tracked and undoable. If a free offer matches but you press enter for the AI anyway, the assistant is told what the offer found so it does not solve the same problem twice. Two more work the same way: **fix broken links** (records whose place was deleted out from under them: the link is cleared, the record stays) and **delete empty places** (nothing inside, nothing filed in them). You have to type the word "empty" for that one, since a rack of shelves you just numbered is empty by definition. **The plan does not need the AI, so it does not go down with it.** When the workspace has worked out what your sentence means and the AI then cannot be asked (its key rejected, its limit reached for the day, the service down), the reply says so in one line and still carries the plan as the same Do it card, so the day your AI is out is not the day your workspace forgets what it knows how to do. **Several destinations, one card, taken a part at a time.** "Get all the other grocery/ spices out of inventory and into the dedicated sections" is one sentence with two answers, so the card has one section per destination ("1 into Groceries", "2 into a new Spices section"), each listing its records and each with its own **Do this**; **Do all** at the bottom runs whatever is left. A part you press ticks off in place with what it did and its own **Undo**; one that could not run says why where its button was and lets you try it again; and a card you leave half done is still half done after a reload, Undo included. **A seasoning is a spice, by the table's own rule.** With a Spice Rack installed, "get the grocery and spices out of inventory and into their sections" files a "Garlic Seasoning Blend" under Spices even though the word "spice" is nowhere in its name: the plan reads the same vocabulary the scanner routes by (what each installed list says it holds), and a record that fits two lists goes to the more specific one, with the reason on its line ("→ Spices (seasoning, over Groceries)"). What a catalog says the thing is counts too: "Cumin seeds" that a catalog files under Spices go to your Spices list, not to Plants for the word "seeds", while a catalog that only says "Groceries" is an aisle, not an answer, and the name decides. **What it could not place is its own labelled block.** Under the plan, "Left in Inventory, could not tell (6):" lists the records the plan had no word for, each opening its record; up to eight the block is open, past that it is collapsed with the count and opens on a press into a scrollable list, and what pressing enter would do about them sits under the label either way. Nothing is folded behind "and 3 more": the list is the thing you are deciding about. **A command you already taught it is offered as you type.** When what you are typing matches one, a strip appears above the box saying what it would do ("Creates 3 locations, no AI needed") and the command it matched. **Tab** takes it: the work happens straight away, nothing is sent to a model, and the change is recorded and undoable as usual. **Enter** still sends your message to the AI exactly as before, so the quick way is something you choose rather than something that happens to you, and a sentence the AI should handle is never intercepted. This works whether or not AI is connected: a workspace with a model still has no reason to spend a call on something it already knows. **A command you taught it runs with no AI at all.** Under **Configuration → Assistant**, the section **Things this workspace can do on its own** lists commands worked out from times an AI did something for you, under **Could learn**, each shown with the message it came from. **Teach it** keeps one. After that, typing a sentence of that shape into Ask Cobb offers to run it, with a plain description of what it will do ("Creates 3 locations") and a **Do it** button, and no AI is asked at any point. A command can be turned off without being forgotten, or forgotten entirely. What runs is worked out on the server from the command it stored, so a command can only ever do the kind of thing it was taught, and a sentence that does not fit is refused rather than guessed at. One run is capped at 200 records. **What Cobb did for you becomes something your workspace can do on its own.** Every change Cobb makes is recorded alongside the message that asked for it, and one message that produces several changes is kept together as one example. From those, Cobblr works out reusable **commands**: ask for "make rack 1 through 12 in Den" once and it becomes "make rack {from} through {to} in {parent}", which runs again on any numbers and any place with no AI involved. Only examples it can fully explain become commands. If the numbers are not both written in your message, or one message did two unrelated things, nothing is learned, because a command that fires on the wrong sentence would write to your workspace. One command is capped at 200 records, so a single sentence can never run away with your data. **Basic mode tells you what it could not answer.** Every question that falls through to the "I only handle the basics" reply is collected under **Configuration → Assistant**, in a list called **Asked, but not answered**: most asked first, in the wording somebody actually used, with a count when it has come up more than once. **Answer this** opens the answer form with those words already filled in as the trigger phrases, so you only write the reply, and saving takes the question off the list. The tick dismisses one you do not want to answer; if it gets asked again it comes back, because that is your workspace telling you it still matters. Only unanswered questions are recorded, and nothing leaves your workspace. **You can teach basic mode:** under **Configuration → Assistant**, edit the built-in answers, turn any off, or add your own, give it trigger words ("returns, refund, send it back") and the reply Cobb should give. There's a live **"try it"** box so you can see exactly what a question matches. Matching is by keyword (no AI), and it's per-workspace.

A cloud AI-plan limitation is stated once on the screen. When you open Cobb beside a page with the same notice, the notice stays in Cobb. Closing the panel restores the page notice. A different restriction, such as where photo identification is available, keeps its own explanation.

**More than one of your AI connections in one workspace.** You can route several, and then you choose which one goes first: the workspace row on your connection says which of yours is already powering that workspace, and offers to put this one first or leave the other in front. Before, the one you had edited most recently quietly won, which meant renaming or re-keying a connection could change which model answered in a workspace you had not touched. Nothing falls through to a second connection when the first one errors or hits its daily cap: the call fails and says so, because silently spending a different key (possibly a paid one) is not a decision Cobblr should make for you. The order is also recorded per kind of work, so a fast model can serve the live camera scan while a slower, better one handles inbox identification and chat.

**Which model: latest, or a version you pin.** A Claude or OpenAI connection lists the models your own key can use, asked of the provider itself, so a new model appears in the pickers (a job's default under **Configuration → AI**, and the model pill in Cobb) on the day the provider offers it, with no Cobblr update. For Claude, each family comes first as **latest**, with the version it means today in brackets: "Haiku · latest (5.5)". That is the default, and it moves by itself: when a newer Haiku appears, calls go to it, and the activity feed says so once ("Haiku latest now means Haiku 5.6"). Under **Pin a version** are the exact versions ("Haiku 5.5", "Haiku 4.5"): a pinned version never moves on its own. If the provider stops offering a version you pinned, calls go to the newest of that family and the activity feed says so once. The list is asked again when you open a picker and it is over an hour old, and **Refresh models** beside the model asks now. If the provider cannot be asked, the picker shows the usual models and says so. **Upgrading moved existing choices to latest:** a job or a Cobb pill that was set to a Claude model before latest existed now follows its family's latest, and the activity feed says it once, for example "Now using Haiku 5.5. Pin 4.5 in Configuration → AI if you want it back." A call's cost is worked out from that model's own price; a model whose price is not known yet shows **price unknown** in the AI log rather than $0.

**AI sharing (using a member's AI in a workspace).** You set up a personal AI once under **Your account → Connections** (`/me/connections`) and route it to the workspaces you choose. For a workspace you **own**, routing it on works immediately, with no reload (Cobb, the scanner and the AI settings page all see the change as soon as you save it, and removing a connection takes it away as promptly): if that workspace has no AI yet, this becomes its AI, because ticking the workspace is what turning it on there means. If the workspace already has one, yours is routed and ready, and the AI its owner picked keeps serving until you switch under **Configuration → AI sharing** (that is also how you get a workspace back if its AI ever goes away: open the connection and save it again). For a workspace you're only a **member** of, choosing **Share** turns it into an **offer the workspace owner approves**: until they do, it powers only *your own* calls, not the workspace's Ask Cobb for everyone. Both sides get clear signals so an offer never sits silently:

- **The sharer** sees each routed workspace's state on `/me/connections` (*awaiting owner approval*, *approved*, or *active*) so you know whether your share is actually doing anything.
- **The owner** gets a notification with **Approve** and **Deny** on it, in the bell and in a Discord DM if they use one, **and an email**, plus a **dashboard callout** ("*X offered to share their AI: Approve / Decline*") and an amber dot on **Configuration**. Only the owner decides this (it is somebody's own account paying for the calls), so an admin is not asked. Reopened after it was decided, the notification says what was decided instead of offering the buttons again, and if the member stops sharing first it stops asking. Approving it (one tap; it auto-becomes the workspace's active AI if none is set) turns on Ask Cobb and the other AI features for the whole workspace. Manage or switch it anytime under **Configuration → AI sharing** (owner only).

**Template**: A string used by some actions to format their output. Syntax: `{{key}}` and `{{key | default: "fallback"}}`. Templates can read entity fields directly (`{{name}}`, `{{qty}}`) PLUS namespaced event-payload fields (`{{event.delta}}`, `{{event.reason}}`) PLUS actor metadata (`{{event.actor.display_name}}`). The renderer HTML-escapes everything (safety against pasted bundles).

**Pairings**: Polymorphic relationships between entities. `(source_module, source_type, source_id) → (target_module, target_type, target_id)` with a `relationship_kind`. The basis for both the dependency system (Task `depends_on` Part) and any cross-module link a builder needs. Pairings + traits together solve the N²-modules-knowing-about-each-other problem.

**Links** (what pairings look like on a record). Every record, whatever it is (a part, a printer, a project, a list), has **Links**: what it is linked to, read in words from its own side. A mod says *installed on Ender 3*; the printer says *has installed Direct drive conversion*; a part says *used by* the mod. Tap **Link**, say how they are linked, and tap the other thing: a table can declare the links its records make (the **Printer mods** bundle declares *installed on* and *uses part*), and picking one lists the printers or parts straight away, so there is nothing to type. Anything else is *related to*, found by search. The same link twice is refused and says so, a thing cannot be linked to itself, and removing a link leaves both things where they were. The assistant links, changes a link's note and unlinks the same way ("this manual is for the drill press"), and each can be undone.

**Activity log**: Append-only audit trail. Every meaningful event lands here; failures from the wire engine end up as `wire_failed` rows that show on the /wires page.

Some questions do not need a model. As you type one Cobb can answer from your own records, the answer appears in a **green bubble**, before you press enter: counts ("how many locations do I have"), where something is ("where is my drill"), what needs you, and what is low on stock. Green because it is a different kind of answer from the assistant's, and it says so: **straight from your workspace, no AI used**. It works with no AI connected at all, since nothing is asked of one. It is deliberately narrow, because an answer shown unprompted must never be a guess: a word that is not one of your record types, a noun matching two of them, or anything that would change something is left alone and goes to the assistant when you press enter, exactly as before.

While Cobb writes, the words appear in the panel as he produces them, under the list of what he has already done. A reply that takes ten seconds starts showing after about three, so a long answer stops looking like a hang. Streaming happens when the provider supports it and the turn is a plain answer rather than one that is calling tools, since a half-arrived tool call is not something to show. **Self-hosting with the Claude bridge?** It now streams from the CLI for real. Older bridges waited for the whole answer and then sent it in a single piece, which looked like streaming to the app and like a long silence to you, so update the bridge to get this.

On a list, ticking rows is how you say "these". On a record's own page there is nothing to tick, so its actions now include a small Cobb button: press it and that record is in context, the chat opens ready for your question, and the chip shows its name. Press it again to drop it. The button lights amber while that record is the one in context, which is the same amber used to highlight text you selected, so both read as the same idea. It appears on every detail page that shows record actions, including ones added later.

When you ask for a set rather than a single thing, Cobb makes the whole set in one go: "each rack should have Shelf 1 through 5", "bins A through H in the workshop". You get one line in the chat saying how many were added, with one Undo beside it that puts all of them back (it counts out loud as it works, since putting back sixty records takes a moment). Anything that was already there is counted rather than duplicated, and said plainly: "Added 58 locations. 2 were already there." Up to 200 records in a single request.

Under **Configuration → Assistant**, a section called **Asked, but not answered** lists the questions basic mode had no reply for, most asked first, with the wording someone actually used and a count when it has come up more than once. **Answer this** opens the answer form with those words already filled in as the trigger phrases, so you only write the reply; saving it takes the question off the list. The tick dismisses a question you do not want to answer, and if somebody asks it again it comes back, because that is the workspace telling you it still matters. Nothing here leaves your workspace, and only questions that got no answer are recorded.

Actions you bound to a kind ride the rows. Say once that printing a label applies to everything physical, and a Print label button appears on every one of those rows, not only on each record's own page. A module's actions that you have not bound stay on the record's page, where there is room to explain them; up to three of your own bound actions show per row. Anything that cannot be put right again asks before it runs, because a row button sits between an edit pencil and a delete bin: the first tap says "sure?" and the second one does it. Saying where an action applies changes what everyone in the workspace is offered, so only an owner or admin can change it or put it back; the same holds for the settings panels a hosted workspace adds (billing, a chat integration), unless a panel says members may use it.

Three kinds of answer arrive before you press enter, each labelled in the bubble:

- **from the page you are on** - "what page am I on?", "where am I". The browser already knows, so nothing is asked of anyone.
- **straight from your workspace** - "how many parts do I have?", "what's low on stock?". One read of your records, no AI.
- **from what Cobb already knows** - "what can you do?", "how do I add a part", "where do I scan?". Cobb's built-in answers, which you can edit and add to under Configuration.

Press enter only if you want the full answer from AI. If the green bubble already said it, you have saved the call.

Ask Cobb to change a field the same way you would ask a person:

- "rename the Colour field on parts to Shade"
- "make Purchase Date required"
- "add Aran to the yarn weight choices"
- "remove Shelf Life from parts"

"Make Purchase Date required" and "add Aran to the yarn weight choices" work the same with no AI connected: Cobb offers the change as a command you can run, and with AI connected that offer rides beside the answer. A sentence that only points ("make it required") is left for the AI to read, since what "it" means is on your screen, not in the words.

He finds the field by the label you see on the form, and asks which you meant if two are close. Removing a field takes it off forms and lists; anything already recorded in it is kept, so adding the field back under the same name brings those values into view again.

One thing he will not do: change a field that belongs to a whole class of records rather than one kind. Those live on the Fields screen, because a change to one lands on every record in the class.

Some fields belong together and go on in one go. Ask for the set rather than the fields:

- "track where my things came from" switches on Provenance: where each thing came from, when, and what it cost, on every physical thing the workspace tracks, now and in future.
- "turn provenance off" takes those fields away again and keeps anything already recorded in them, so switching it back on brings the values into view.

Asking for the set is not the same as building three fields by hand with those names. The set's fields carry the roles that let a scanned receipt fill them in; hand-made ones look identical on the form and stay empty.

Renaming a heading on a form works the same way as grouping: "rename the Buying heading to Purchasing".

A kind's custom fields can sit under named headings on its form, and you can say so rather than dragging:

- "put purchase date and supplier under Buying"
- "group cost and supplier together under Buying"
- "take supplier out of Buying"

Name the fields the way they read on the form. The order you name them is the order they get under the heading, and the heading is created if it does not exist yet. Taking fields out puts them back in the ungrouped list, and a heading with nothing left under it is removed rather than left as an empty line.

A field that applies to a whole class of records (everything physical, say) is not grouped this way: its place on the form belongs to the class rather than to one kind, so those stay on the Fields screen.

Cobb can rearrange your navigation. "Put Spices and Tea under Kitchen" is a change he proposes and you confirm, like any other: he matches the names to the sections you actually have, creates the heading if it does not exist, and moves them under it. A section belongs to one heading at a time, so this takes it out of any other. If a name matches two sections he asks which you meant rather than moving the wrong one, and if it matches none he says what your navigation does have. "Take Tea back out" puts it at the top level again.

The small Cobb button beside a record's other actions puts THAT record into the chat's context and opens the panel ready for your question, and it now sits wherever the record does: on its own page, and on each row of a list that shows one. On Locations that means every area and every container, so you can ask about one rack without ticking it first or naming it in your message. Press it again to drop the context; it stays lit amber while it is the one in play.

Three more things you can just ask for:

- "call my parts spools" renames what that kind of record is called everywhere it is shown: the navigation, headings, buttons. It is the label only. Nothing recorded moves, no id changes, and renaming it back is the whole undo. Say the plural too when it is not the plain plus-s ("call a Box a Bin, several Bins").
- "give spices their own list" takes one category out of a list and gives it a list of its own, with its own nav entry, fields and label codes. The records keep their ids, their history and their printed labels, so the code on the bin still resolves; only which list they are in changes.
- "fold spices back into the pantry" is the exact reverse, stamping everything with the category you name as it goes back.

Deleting a whole list is still yours to do on the screen, because folding it back is the version that keeps your records and deleting is the version that does not.

While Cobb works, the panel lists what he has done, a step at a time, with the one in progress carrying a clock. That now holds however your AI is connected. When your assistant is a personal connection that runs the tools itself, each of its changes appears in that list as it happens, named, and ticks green when it lands, so a long request reads as work with its reasons visible rather than a spinner. A step that failed is marked rather than dropped.

Ask for a feature the way it is written in the app:

- "turn on purchases"
- "I want to track maintenance"
- "turn off shipments, I don't use it"

Turning one on is safe to repeat: a feature already on is left alone and he says so. Turning one off takes its screens out of your navigation and keeps the records already stored, so switching it back on brings them back. The fields and automations that came with the feature are cleared out, though, so anything you tuned there is set up again from scratch. If another feature you use depends on the one you asked to remove, he refuses and names it.

When what you are typing into Ask Cobb matches a command your workspace already knows, a strip appears above the box: what it would do ("Creates 3 locations, no AI needed") and the command it matched. **Tab** takes it. The work happens straight away, nothing is sent to a model, and the change is recorded and undoable like any other. **Enter** still sends your message to the AI exactly as before, so the quick way is something you choose rather than something that happens to you, and a sentence meant for the AI is never intercepted. It works whether or not AI is connected, because a workspace with a model still has no reason to spend a call on something it already knows. Nothing is offered until you have taught it at least one command, and nothing is suggested for a message under eight characters.

Some jobs cannot be taught as a phrase with blanks to fill in, because what they do depends on what is there. "Delete duplicates" is one: Cobb looks for places with the same name in the same spot, says exactly what he would remove and what he would keep, and waits for you to accept. It needs no AI. If you have ticked or highlighted something first, that scopes the search, so with a rack selected it means the shelves in that rack. What it removes is tracked and undoable like any other change, and asking again when there is nothing to find says so rather than offering an empty change. When a free offer matches but you press enter for the AI anyway, the assistant is told what the offer found, as information: it can do the same thing, or explain why it is doing something else.

Cobb can clear duplicates in any list whose module has said what counts as "the same place" for it. Places are scoped by what they are inside, parts by the bin they are filed in. So two "Shelf 1"s in the same rack are offered for removal, and the same two in different racks are left alone. Kinds that have not declared a reading, like assets, are never deduplicated at all: two of the same name there are usually two real things. The offer names each kind separately ("1 duplicate location, 1 duplicate part") so you can see what it means before accepting.

Every change Cobb makes is now recorded alongside **the message that asked for it**, and one message that produces several changes is kept together as a single example. From those, Cobblr works out reusable **commands**: "make rack 1 through 12 in Den" becomes "make rack {from} through {to} in {parent}", which runs again on any numbers and any place, with no AI involved. Only examples it can fully explain become commands: if the numbers you asked for are not both written in your message, or one message did two unrelated things, nothing is learned, because a command that fires on the wrong sentence would write to your workspace. A single command is capped at 200 records so one sentence can never run away with your data.

Modules ship commands of their own, so **Configuration → Assistant** already lists a few under **Things this workspace can do on its own** before you have taught it anything, each labelled with the module it came from. Locations ships "make {label} {from} through {to} in {parent}", which numbers a run of shelves, bays or bins inside a place you name, and "add a place called {name} in {parent}". They work with no AI connected at all. A command your workspace learned for itself has an **export** that gives you the snippet to contribute it back, so if it turns out to be something everybody wants it can ship in the module rather than being taught again in every workspace. Nothing is uploaded: the export is text for you to read and share if you choose. Creating a place now also accepts the parent **by name** ("in Garage"), and says so plainly when no place by that name exists, instead of quietly creating it at the top level.

Basic mode now follows the conversation. When Cobb offers a command ("I can do that: **make rack 1 through 5 in Den**"), typing **yes** or **go for it** runs it, the same as pressing Do it. After a command runs, **again** repeats it, and **undo** puts its changes back, the same as the Undo button on the message. **Stop** or **never mind** waves off an offer you did not want. All of it works with no AI connected; nothing new can be triggered this way, only the command that was already offered or already ran, through the same guarded paths as the buttons.

Ask Cobb can be pointed at things. Tick the checkboxes on a list, or highlight some text anywhere on the page, and a chip appears above the message box saying what is in context ("12 locations", "Selected text"). Send your message and Cobb gets those records themselves, with their ids, so "delete duplicates" or "move these to the Garage" means exactly what you picked. The × on the chip drops it, and sending clears it, so the next question starts clean. Highlighting is captured the moment you make it, so clicking into the box (which normally clears a highlight) does not lose it.

**Configuration → Assistant** grows a section called **Things this workspace can do on its own**. Under **Could learn** are commands worked out from times an AI did something for you: "make rack 1 through 12 in Den" becomes **make rack {from} through {to} in {parent}**, shown with the message it came from. **Teach it** keeps it. After that, typing a sentence of that shape into Ask Cobb offers to run it, with a plain description of what it will do ("Creates 3 locations") and a **Do it** button, and no AI is asked at any point. Commands can be turned off without being forgotten, or forgotten entirely. What actually runs is worked out on the server from the command it stored, so a command can only ever do the kind of thing it was taught, and a sentence that does not fit is refused. A command is one instruction: a message that gives two ("make Shelf 1 through 3 in Audit room then add a place called Blue bin in Shelf 1") is not offered as one, and Cobb lists the steps to send one at a time. One run is capped at 200 records.

Tick rows on any list and the chip above the message box says what is in context: "12 parts", "3 machines", "2 orders". Your message carries those records themselves, so "order more of these" means the ones you picked rather than a search. This works on Parts, Assets, Machines, Projects, Purchases and Locations. A few screens select things that are not records yet, like the scan inbox, and those deliberately do not put anything in context.

Alongside "delete duplicates", Cobb can do two more chores without an AI. **Fix broken links** finds records whose place has been deleted out from under them and clears the dead link; the records stay, only the link goes. **Delete empty places** finds places with nothing inside and nothing filed in them. Both show you the list before doing anything, both are undoable, and both go quiet when there is nothing to find. The second needs the word "empty" in what you type, because a rack of shelves you just numbered is empty by definition and nobody wants those offered back.

Undo takes back what Cobb did, not what you did afterwards. If nothing else has touched the record, it goes back exactly. If you edited it in the meantime, only the parts still as Cobb left them are put back and the panel names what it kept ("Left the notes as you set it"). If you changed the very field Cobb changed, the undo declines and says why, since your edit is the more recent one. Undoing a create will not delete a record you have since renamed or filed things into, and undoing an edit will never bring back a record you have deleted. Each of those refusals is a sentence explaining what to do instead, and nothing is ever silently reverted on your behalf.

When an undo leaves something alone, it says which and why, and offers to take that back too. Cobb never decides to overwrite your own later edit, but he does put the option in front of you: "Left Shelf 3 alone, because you have changed it since" with a button beside it. Pressing that goes through with the parts that were held back, including deleting a container along with what is still inside it, and it is recorded like any other change, so you can undo the undo.

Every change Cobb makes records the record's whole state before and after, so undo is a restore, not a reverse operation. A deleted record comes back as the same record, with the same id, so children, labels and anything filed under it still point at it. An edit is put back in full, not just the fields the change happened to name. If someone (or something) else changed that record after Cobb did, the undo tells you it rolled that back too, and can itself be undone. And a whole instruction is one thing to take back: the Undo beside "Added 58 locations" puts all 58 back in a single request.

**Paste a picture wherever a photo can be added.** Copy a picture or take a screenshot, then press Ctrl or Cmd plus V: it goes where you would otherwise have chosen a file. That works on a record's photo, a part's gallery, a knowledge entry's image, a design's files, a record's Files list, **Add photos** on the dashboard, the scan inbox, the camera sheet when there is no camera, the Files page, the theme's logo, the feedback form and the picture viewer. A picture's web address pasted into **Find a photo on the web** (or the viewer) is used as that picture. With two places on one page, the picture goes to the one you last clicked or tapped, and a dialog on top takes it before the page behind it does. Pasting text into a text box is never taken as a picture.

**Paste a file the same way, where files are taken.** A PDF, a spreadsheet or any document copied in your file manager pastes like a picture, wherever the upload button would take it: on a record, a picture goes to its photos and anything else (a bill of sale, a manual) to its Files, with no question asked; in the scan inbox, anywhere on the page or into the box at the top, a picture goes to photo intake and a PDF or CSV is read as a receipt ("Reading <file name>…"). A copied file also carries its name as text, and the name is never what is pasted: the file is. A paste takes exactly the files the upload button beside it takes.

**A file is shown as what it is.** A PDF shows its first page as its picture, in the scan inbox and in a record's Files, whether or not it could be read; if a page cannot be drawn (an encrypted PDF), the tile shows the file's name instead, never a blank. Tap it to open the PDF reader. A document that came into the inbox is named by what it is: a file you uploaded keeps its own name ("Bill of Sale.pdf"), and an emailed receipt is "Email from eBay, Aug 12" (the shop, or the address it came from when the shop could not be read). While the document needs nothing, it is a small chip in its session's header ("eBay email · 1 line", with a tiny first page for a PDF or a photo) that opens it; the full document card, with where its reading stands in words ("Couldn't read yet"), appears only when it needs you: it could not be read, it read into no lines, or it is waiting for the AI. A session says once, in its header, who added it when it was not you: **Added by Claude** (the name of the assistant's connection), **Added by Cobb**, an API token's name, or **Emailed from** the address a receipt was forwarded from; tap it for when and through which connection. A card repeats it only when its own source differs from its session's. A code read off an emailed receipt says **read from the email**, never "read from photo". What a file is comes from its bytes, not from the type it arrived with, so a PDF stored under the wrong type is still opened as a PDF (and put right the first time it is opened), and **View original** never shows a file's raw bytes as text: a file that is not text says so and offers **Download**.

**A scanned PDF is read from its page.** A PDF from a scanner has no text in it, only a photo of the paper, so Cobblr reads that photo the way it reads a photo of a receipt, and the inbox card says **Scanned PDF, read from its image**. When a read fails, the message is about what you gave it: a PDF is never told to check the picture.

**A bill of sale is one thing and its purchase.** A bill of sale (a car, a boat, a used machine, from a person or a dealer) is read by what the form prints, with no AI: it becomes one item named for the thing ("2019 Honda Civic Hatchback EX") at the unit price, on the date of sale, sold by the seller (or the dealer named at the top), never the buyer. A vehicle's VIN, make, model and year fill its own fields the way scanning the VIN does, and the odometer reading fills Mileage when it is empty. If you already have that vehicle, the card says so, found by its VIN even when you named it something else. The inbox card says **Bill of sale, read** (or **read from its scan** for a scanned PDF).

**A scanned bill of sale is checked before it is believed.** A full page (a scanned PDF, or a photo of a whole sheet) is read the way a form is: the paper's tint (yellow carbon copy) is taken out, the page is straightened, the ruled boxes are removed, and each box is read on its own with only the characters it can hold. Then the page's own arithmetic decides whether to believe the read: the VIN's check digit, the year against the VIN, the lines adding up to the taxable amount and the total, and the deposit being the total less the balance on delivery. A VIN that passes is the authority for the vehicle: the model year comes from the VIN itself, and the make, model and name from the vehicle database, so a misread model line still gives "2019 Honda Civic Hatchback EX", and the vehicle you already have is offered as **It's this one**. When a check fails, the page goes to your AI's document reader (a stronger model than the one that names photos, never a lighter one), and its answer is checked the same way; when every AI is busy, the item waits for one. Whatever still does not add up is said on the card ("Please check the price and the total: the page did not read clearly"), and a name that is not words is never searched for a picture. **Looks fine** clears it once you have looked.

**A bill of sale for something you already have goes on it.** However the card found the thing (its VIN, its name, "Is this the same one?"), a bill of sale for it is its paperwork, never a second one: the card asks **Is this the paperwork for …?** and **It's this one** puts the document on the record, links the purchase with all its lines, and fills the record's empty fields by meaning: Paid, Purchased at, Bought from, and the VIN, mileage, year, make and model. No "+1 more" is offered, and the count stays as it was unless the paper itself sold several. A field that already holds something is left alone and offered as "says X · Use it". A value that failed its check (a seller read as a sentence of the small print, a make read across the form's captions) is never written anywhere: it is shown under **Please check**, with what was read and a box to correct it, and the field stays empty. When the document AI cannot read the page right now, the card says so plainly ("The AI's daily limit is used up; it reads this again later") and the page waits for it rather than being filled from the misread.

**Fees and taxes are charges, not things.** On any receipt, a fee, a tax, a title or registration charge, a documentary or processing fee, delivery, a warranty or an insurance policy is kept as a line on the purchase and never becomes an inbox item or goes looking for a photo. The purchase shows it marked **charge**, and its total still includes it. An extra on a bill of sale that is a thing (wheel locks) stays on the purchase as well, with **Make it an item** beside it: press it to put it in the scan inbox, where filing it links it to that line.

**Looking at pictures: one viewer everywhere.** Tap any picture that can open bigger, and it opens in the same viewer wherever it is: a feedback report's screenshots, a record's photo, a part's or a design's gallery, the scan inbox, the Files page, a file's own page, a print's camera frame, a picture a service made. When the picture belongs to a set, the viewer steps through all of it: **← and →** on a keyboard, the **‹ ›** buttons at the sides, a **swipe** on a phone, or the strip of thumbnails underneath, and it says where you are ("2 of 5"). Tap the picture to see it at its own size and scroll around it, so a label or a serial number reads. **Esc** closes it (or the ✕, or a tap on the dark surround), and the keyboard goes back to the thumbnail you opened it from. On a record's photo the viewer also offers **Change photo**; under the photo, **Change**, **Auto** and **Remove** are still one tap away.

**Links.** Every record has **Links**: what it is linked to, read from its own side
(*installed on Ender 3* on the mod, *has installed* on the printer, *used by* on a part).
Tap **Link**, pick how they are linked, then tap the other thing. A table can declare
its own links (the **Printer mods** bundle declares *installed on* and *uses part*), and
picking one lists what it can point at with nothing to type; anything else is *related
to*, found by search. The same link twice is refused, and removing a link keeps both
things.

Tick the items in the scan inbox, open Cobb and say what the list should be called and that these go in it, for example "create Mugs and file these in it". The card shows two steps: making the list (it says which existing kind it is kept like, and the fields that come with it) and filing the items you ticked. Press Confirm once and both happen. The done card's Undo files the items back into the inbox and takes the list away again while it is empty. A list with nothing in it shows "Nothing is filed in it yet" on its page with a Remove this list button. The line and the button go the moment the first thing is filed in it, however it got there, with no reload; every count of what a list, place or collection holds reads again after any change.

Choose Edit beside a list in Presentation to change its icon, hide it from navigation, choose its navigation order, or nest it under its module. A module's default list also offers a Categories heading. For a list, One of them is called and More than one set the words used for its records and New button.

Cobb can make the same changes. Try “hide Bookshelf from navigation”, “give my tools the wrench icon”, or “nest Spices under Inventory”. A saved presentation change offers Undo, which restores its previous settings. Clearing a text setting restores its default; settings omitted from an assistant request stay as they are.

**Two people changing the same thing.** A record's page saves a field when you leave it or press Enter, and it never writes over a change someone else made after you opened it. If a teammate, another tab or Cobb changes the field while you are typing in it, your Enter saves nothing and the field says what it holds now: *Changed to "Lathe in bay 2" after you opened this, so nothing was saved.* Save again to put yours in its place, or press Escape to keep theirs. A field you are not typing in simply shows the new value. A tab left open since before an update of the app saves nothing from its forms and asks you to reload the page, so its older copy never replaces newer changes.

**Undo a field you saved.** Every field a record's page saves (when you leave it or press Enter) shows **Saved.** with **Undo** for a few seconds, and Undo puts back what the field held before. It still works after you close the record or go to another page, as long as the message is on screen. Several fields saved on one record in a row are one Undo that puts all of them back. If someone has changed the field since, Undo leaves their change and says who made it.

## 3. Modules

In Configuration → Modules, a retired module stays available while your
workspace has it enabled. Its notice explains why it has been retired.
Disabling it keeps your data, but you cannot enable it again while it is
retired. Export anything you need before disabling it. A module with an
alternate name still has one installation and the same records.

## 4. The platform layer

Everything below this point is **shared infrastructure** that lives in the platform itself, not in any module. It's why modules can compose at all.

### 4.1 Entity kinds

Every module declares its entity kinds in its manifest. At boot, the platform mirrors them into a registry. The /wires and /fields pages both read from this registry to populate dropdowns.

```ts
// in modules/<module>/src/module.ts
provides: {
  entityKinds: [
    {
      id: "inventory:part",
      displayName: "Part",
      icon: "boxes",
      fields: [
        { name: "name", role: "title", type: "text", required: true },
        { name: "qty", type: "number" },
        // ...
      ],
    },
  ],
},
```

`role: "title"` is the signal other modules use to find the "name-y" field. That's how labels:print knows what to print without inventory telling it directly.

You can introspect this from the API:

```bash
curl -H "Authorization: Bearer $TOKEN" \
  http://localhost:4000/api/v1/orgs/$SLUG/entity-kinds | jq '.items[].id'
# "core-locations:location"
# "inventory:part"
# "machines:machine"
# "assets:asset"
# "projects:project"
# "projects:task"
# "purchases:order"
# "purchases:order_item"
```

### 4.2 Actions + the EntityActionsBar

Actions are like buttons that a module *donates* to the platform. The platform decides which ones to show on which entity based on a small predicate (`appliesTo`). Three predicates are supported today:

- `{ any: true }`: applies to any kind.
- `{ kinds: ["foo:bar", "baz:qux"] }`: exact-kind allowlist.
- `{ hasFieldRole: "title" }`: any kind whose manifest declares a field with that role.

When you open a part detail page, the **EntityActionsBar** at the top fetches `/actions?kind=inventory:part`, filters to installed modules, and renders a button for each applicable action. Click it, the platform invokes the module-declared handler with `{ orgId, entityKind, entityId }`. The handler does its thing.

```bash
# {
#   "items": [
#     { "id": "labels:print", "label": "Print label", ... },
#     { "id": "projects:set-dep-satisfied", ... }
#   ],
#   "bindings": []
# }
```

**When a thing is no longer yours.** On an asset, a machine or an item, **No longer mine…** under its buttons asks what happened: sold, given away, parted out, converted, traded, lost, or thrown out, the day, the price when it was sold, and who has it now. Saving it:

- takes it out of the list and its totals: an asset leaves the register and its "paid X in all";
- keeps the record whole, with its history, photos and printed labels, findable in search and under **Gone** (machines), **Gone or put away** (assets) or **Archived only** (items);
- puts one line at the top of its page: *Sold 2026-09-12 for $180 to a neighbour · kept 3 years 2 months* ("kept" is counted from its acquired date when it has one).

**Bring back** on that line makes it yours again, back in the list in the state it left from. Picking **sold** or **given away** in an asset's State does the same as **No longer mine…**, and picking a state it is still yours in brings it back. The assistant takes it in words: "I sold the old Ender 3 for $120", "I gave the drill press to my brother", "I got the drill press back".

**When a thing is lent out.** On an asset, a machine or an item, **Lend…** asks who has it (someone on your People list, or any name), why (**repair**, **borrowed**, or your own words), since when and, if you like, when it is due back. Nothing else about it changes: it keeps its place, which is where it goes back to, and its state. Its page says *With Sam Lee · repair · since Oct 13, 2026 (due Oct 27, 2026) · returns to Front Hallway*, marked **overdue** once the day has passed.

While it is out:

- **Move**, **Back where it was**, moving its stock and **Print label** are greyed, and each says *Lent to Sam Lee.*;
- a lent printer gets no jobs from the print farm, and its tile on the fleet says who has it;
- its service reminders wait, and come back when it does;
- its due day is on the calendar, and Needs you shows it due soon, due today or overdue. With no due day, Needs you mentions it once it has been out a month.

**It's back** on that line ends the loan. If the loan changed while the return was waiting, it refuses the return and asks you to review the current loan. Back from a repair it asks **Was it repaired?**; a yes puts *Repaired by Sam Lee* in its service log, with what was done. History keeps the borrower, loan dates and note after return, including any deposit terms you saved. Tap or click a long note to read it in full. Undo puts it back out. On an asset, **Who has it** and **Due back** follow the loan. The assistant takes it in words: "I dropped the Dremel off with Sam to fix it, back in two weeks", "the ladder is back, the clip is fixed", "what have I lent out?".

### Two people editing one record

A form saves only what you changed in it. If someone else, or you in another tab, saved a different field of the same record while your form was open, both changes stay. If they changed the same field, your save is refused and says who changed it and when; nothing of yours is saved. Look at the record again and save what you still want. This holds on every record's page, in a list's cells, on a board you drag a card across, for an Undo on a floor plan, and for a change you made offline that is sent when the connection comes back.
