# Changelog

User-facing changes, newest first. Dates are release dates.

## 2026-07-09

### Features
- Organize what you already track. The scan page's new Organize header button points the put-away planner at your *committed* things that have no location yet: everything Cobblr tracks but doesn't know a home for, grouped and routed to bins the same way a scanned pile is (evidence, proposed new bins, per-group accept, the put-away walk). Homeless inventory gets homes without re-scanning anything.
- Organize a whole pile at once. Select 2+ scanned items and hit Organize…: Cobblr groups them by what they are and proposes where each group should go: an existing bin where similar items already live (with the evidence shown), a sensibly-named new bin, or "needs a destination" when there's no defensible answer. Accept per group or all at once; nothing moves until you say so, and anything you already filed by hand stays put. Works without AI too, grouped by where siblings live. Then Start put-away walk for the physical half: bin by bin, each destination becomes your active filing bin and you scan (hardware scanner) or tap items off the checklist: progress saves, so a Resume put-away walk chip picks up where you left off.
- Homebox is now a live sync connector. Alongside the CSV import, you can connect a self-hosted Homebox instance under Configuration → Integrations → Live sync → + Add connection → Homebox: paste its URL + an API key. It brings your items across whole (name, quantity, cost, serial, model, manufacturer, warranty, custom fields, and each item's photo), rebuilds your location hierarchy, and files each item into its location. Import once, or leave the sync on to keep mirroring; matched by Homebox id so a re-run updates in place. (A quick heads-up for the curious: Homebox's API lists items in summary form and keeps locations as a tree, so the connector re-fetches each item's detail and flattens the tree; you don't need to think about any of that.)
- Switching from Homebox? There's now a one-stop migration under Configuration → Integrations → "Migrate in from another app". Pick Homebox, upload its CSV export, preview exactly what will happen, and import: your items become Inventory parts (with serial, model, warranty, cost and all), the location paths rebuild your Locations hierarchy, and labels become tags. Anything Cobblr has no column for is kept safely in each item's details, so nothing is lost. It lives right beside the live sync connectors (Ravelry), because Homebox's CSV is its most reliable interface. (Photos aren't in Homebox's CSV, a follow-up will bring those.)
- One-click "Import from Homebox (live)." Under Configuration → Integrations → *Migrate in from another app*, a new Homebox: live card imports your whole Homebox in one step, paste your Homebox address + an API token, and it brings your locations, items, custom fields, and photos across (photos are the thing the CSV export can't carry). When it's done it asks whether to keep it synced (future Homebox changes mirror automatically) or import just this once: either way the connection is saved under Live sync, so you can re-run it, turn on syncing later, or archive it anytime. A Homebox on your local network is reached through your workspace's edge bridge. The old CSV import is still there as Homebox, CSV for when you'd rather not use the API.
- Whole-house floor plans: floors as tabs, rooms that zoom. A building's page now shows one plan per floor behind tabs ("Main floor" / "Basement"; "+ floor" creates them sized). Rooms draw on their floor as visible rectangles; clicking one zooms into the room's own plan in place, fully editable, without losing the house view. A toolbox's laid-out face zooms the same way. Dropping something inside a room re-files it there and lands it on the room's plan at the same physical spot.
- Floor plans: draw a space to scale, and let it file things for you. A location's detail page can now hold a real 2D plan: the room as a rectangle (dimensions in whatever unit you think in: "33ft", "7.3m"), interior walls with door cutouts, and every rack/toolbox/bench placed where it stands. Each rectangle IS a storage location: click to open it, drag it into a bay and it re-files there. Zones show as faint toggleable labels, never chrome. Containers get the same treatment in elevation, a toolbox's face lays out its drawers at true scale, partial-width ones visibly partial. Or skip the drawing: describe the space in a sentence and the AI drafts the plan for you to true up. The day-to-day view shows no numbers at all, measurements live in the data and appear only while editing.
- OpenRouter, first-class: one key, any model. Add an OpenRouter connection (workspace AI or your personal AI connections): paste your key, name a model (`anthropic/claude-sonnet-5`, `openai/gpt-5.5`, …), done, no base URL to know, and switching models later is editing one field. The generic OpenAI-compatible provider gains the same optional Model field, so vLLM and other exact-name servers work on a personal connection too (LM Studio still needs nothing). And Ask Cobb's create-from-chat got honest and smarter: modules now *declare* which records can be created from chat (knowledge entries and tracking metrics join in), Cobb is told each kind's real field names so a saved pattern lands with a proper `title` instead of erroring, and it never offers a create that would fail on confirm.
- The floor plan works for you now: scan into, heat, print. Every plan's toolbar gained three buttons: scan into opens the camera already filing into that space (rooms' zoom views arm directly: "I'm standing at the workbench"), heat shades each box by how much is stored in it (rooms count their whole subtree), and print opens a paper-white plan in a new tab, one page per floor for a whole building.
- Tell a bin what it is: pair its QR with the product's UPC. Scan a bin's QR label to make it the active bin, then scan the container's own product barcode (a "Sterilite 6qt", say) and tap "This is ‹bin› itself" on the result card. The product identity lands on the bin's own location record, brand/product under its details, the catalog photo as its picture, instead of filing a new item into it.
- Self-hosting gains a fourth TLS mode: `COBBLR_TLS_MODE=tsnet`. The bundled proxy joins your tailnet as its own virtual node and serves Cobblr at `https://cobblr.<tailnet>.ts.net` with a real, auto-renewed certificate. The box needs no Tailscale install and nothing exposed; you paste one reusable auth key into `.env` and `docker compose up`. The blank-`COBBLR_ACME_EMAIL` crash in the DuckDNS/Cloudflare modes is fixed in the same image (the optional line is now dropped when empty).
- Docs now ship with the feature, and publish with the release. Every feature changeset carries its user documentation (`## docs` + `docs_target:`), written in the same PR while the change is fresh, enforced at the merge gate. `scripts/docs-flush.mjs` publishes each blurb into its target doc only once the feature is actually live, so public docs never describe something that hasn't shipped and release-day docs work is zero.
- Sync connections (Ravelry and friends) get a history. A connection you're done with: imported once and don't want it polling, say, can now be Archived instead of removed: it drops out of the Live-sync list into an Archived section below it and stops syncing, but nothing is lost. One click Re-activate puts it back where it left off (its id-map and settings survive). Archive ≠ remove, *Remove* still deletes for good; *Archive* just tucks it away for later.
- Zones: areas inside an area draw as thin dashed outlines, not nested boxes. Bay 1 / Bay 2 / Bay 3 divide a garage. They aren't rooms inside it. A nested *area* in the Locations tree now renders as a featherweight dashed outline with its name pinned to the border line, and its contents stay at full width in the parent's own flow. Dashed = zone, solid = container, so the two grammars read apart at a glance. Pure re-draw: nothing about the hierarchy, scanning, or filing changes.

### Improvements
- Ask Cobb got noticeably smarter and more consistent. Two workspaces used to give very different answers to the same question: one cause was a prompt-delivery bug where the managed AI silently dropped Cobb's workspace instructions (personal-bridge AI kept them), so they now behave the same. Cobb also stops deflecting real questions with "I only manage records"; it answers helpfully (general knowledge included) and *offers* to save the result. And it can now create more kinds of records straight from chat, anything a module exposes, like a knowledge entry, instead of a fixed short list. (Groundwork for full tool-calling, reading your workspace's contents and taking multi-step actions, is written up in docs/design-decisions/ai-chat-tool-calling.md.)

### Fixes
- Cleanup from field-testing the shared-AI flow. The owner's offer email now says Ask Cobb (one last "Ask Cobblr" straggler); a workspace chatting through a direct Ollama provider gets Cobb's workspace instructions again (a prompt-delivery fix had missed that one adapter); resolving an offer clears the stale notification for every owner, not just the one who clicked; and the Edge bridge ● online/offline indicators now also cover URL connections that ride your bridge (LM Studio via bridge transit), not just the dedicated Local-AI connection.
- Saved views open as a real page, and their rows are clickable. Opening a pinned view from the dashboard now lands on a full-page view (`/views/‹id›`) instead of a preview modal floating over the Views configuration list. And every row is now a link: dashboard pinned-view rows, kanban cards, and list rows all click through to the machine/asset/part they name (registry-driven, so instance kinds like 3D Printers resolve too).

## 2026-07-08

### Features
- See what's inside your things. A machine's detail now has an "Installed components" panel: add the parts that live inside it (a printer's mods, a server's PSU/SSD), see them at a glance, and remove them. It's a generic containment panel that will show up on other containers next.
- Scan components straight into a machine or server. Point the camera at a machine or asset's QR label to make it the active bin, then scan each component's barcode. It's identified and dropped *inside* that container automatically. The "scan into a location" flow you know, now for any container.
- Saved views got a real builder, and sorting now works everywhere. Creating or editing a view is now a field-aware form instead of a raw text box: pick filter fields and operators from dropdowns (`is`, `is any of`, `is not`, and numeric comparisons), pick columns and a group-by field, and (new) add sort rows to order a view by any field, ascending or descending, with tie-breaks. "Is any of" lets one filter match several values at once (e.g. machines that are *building* or *rebuilding*). Sorting is now honored by every module's list (machines, assets, and orders join inventory and projects), so you can finally order your things by most-recently-updated, cost, priority, or whatever field matters.

### Improvements
- When a member offers to share their AI with a workspace, it no longer sits unseen. The workspace owner now gets an email as well as the in-app notification, a dashboard callout with Approve / Decline right there, and an amber dot on Configuration: instead of just an easily-missed bell item. And on phones, the header's notification bell finally has a home: the menu now carries a notifications row with an unread badge (and a red dot on the ☰ button when you have unread items), so mobile users can actually reach their inbox. The person doing the sharing also sees, on their AI connections page, whether each share is *awaiting owner approval*, *approved*, or *active*. No more wondering if it went through.
- Your personal edge bridge (the local-AI tunnel) now shows whether it's actually connected: the same green "online" / grey "offline" dot the workspace's 3D-printer and device bridges already use. It appears on each edge-bridge connection in Connections, and, if you run one, as an Edge bridge ● row right in your account menu, so you can glance at it from anywhere instead of guessing why local AI isn't answering. Same liveness signal under the hood, just surfaced by *your* bridge instead of a workspace's.
- Groundwork for tracking what's *inside* your things: a new containment relationship so a component can live inside a machine or server (and a part inside a printer). This step adds the plumbing; the panel to see components and scan them straight into a container arrives in the next update.

## 2026-07-07

### Features
- Adding a printer to a detection service (like PrintGuard) from Cobblr now works for any connection type the detector supports: including Bambu: driven by a small manifest mapping rather than hardcoded per type. Mirror an existing Cobblr machine and it reuses the credentials Cobblr already holds (they stay server-side, never sent to your browser); for a Bambu you pick which printer (serial). For a single-printer connection you can tick "stop polling it in Cobblr" so only the detector talks to that printer, no double-load.
- When an external detector (like PrintGuard) owns a printer, Cobblr now fully stands down for that printer: per printer, so it works even inside a shared multi-printer connection (e.g. one printer of a Bambu farm moved to the detector). Cobblr stops its own AI watch and camera pull for it, won't send it jobs, and marks it *"watched by detector"* on the floor, so the printer's camera is only ever touched by the detector, with no double-load. Its telemetry (status/progress) keeps flowing, so you still see it.
- New "PrintGuard (send frames)" detector option: Cobblr grabs a frame and posts it to PrintGuard for a verdict, instead of PrintGuard pulling the camera itself. This is the path for cloud Cobblr + a LAN camera (or any setup where PrintGuard can't reach the camera but Cobblr can). It uses PrintGuard's new `/classify` endpoint, which ships in PrintGuard 2.3.0, and Test now reads the running version and tells you clearly if the box is too old ("needs PrintGuard ≥ 2.3.0, found 2.2.2") instead of silently doing nothing.
- Ravelry is now a proper sync connector you point at any table. It used to be a fixed card on your profile that only imported into the Yarn bundle. Now it lives with your other integrations (Configuration → Integrations → Add connection → Ravelry), and you choose which inventory table your stash lands in: the flagship Yarn table or one you built yourself. You no longer need the Yarn bundle installed. You get the standard sync tools too (a before/after import preview and ongoing reconcile), and length is stored as Ravelry provides it (metres), with yd/m display left to your unit preference.

## 2026-07-06

### Features
- Print-failure detection can now use a self-hosted detection service you run anywhere: point it at an Obico ML API or PrintGuard box (or any HTTP model on your LAN) and the spaghetti watch scores prints through it. Pick "External detector" as the backend, add the service's URL + token, and go. Nothing is hardcoded: each service is a small manifest, so new ones drop in without a code change.
- Printed QR codes can now outlive this instance. Under QR codes (owner/admin), set a Label base URL: a stable name you control (a domain, a DuckDNS, or a Tailscale name) that forwards the `/qr/…` path to wherever your workspace lives. New codes encode that base instead of the instance's own address, so if you later self-host, change domains, or leave the hosted app, you just re-point the forward and every printed label keeps working. Leave it blank to use the current address; already-printed codes are unaffected.
- Your lists now show the right face on their own. A collection you count and reorder (filament, screws, groceries) keeps its full stock view: quantities, reorder points, allocations. A collection you just catalog (films, books, recipes, anything with no quantities) reads as a lean list of your own fields, with none of the stock machinery cluttering it. The platform decides from your data, so there's nothing to choose; a one-tap Auto / Always track stock / Catalog only control in the list's Settings is there for the rare case you want to override it. Your list also speaks its own words now: name a collection "Films" and it says "New Film", "search films", "No films yet", never "part", with a "What do you call these?" setting to fix the odd plural. Two new saved-view layouts arrive too: a gallery (a poster wall) and a heatmap (a habit-tracker grid).
- The scan inbox can now export itself to a file, not just import one. The new Export button downloads your whole inbox as an interop file (JSON or CSV): photos included, via short-lived no-auth links another instance can fetch. So moving a scan inbox between workspaces or between Cobblr instances is now Export here → Import there: no re-scanning, no database surgery. It round-trips both ways (and still interops with companion app); on re-import Cobblr re-runs its own matchmaker.

### Improvements
- Feedback moves into the sidebar in full-sidebar mode. When you hide the top bar, the Feedback button is no longer a pill floating over your content. It becomes a row at the top of the sidebar foot, in the same "notices" area as the verify-your-account nudge, set apart from the workspace tools below it. Top-bar mode keeps the floating pill (there's no sidebar foot to host it there). Same one-click Send-feedback dialog either way.
- The quick theme toggle can now offer to set your account default too. Flipping a screen to dark (or light) with the header or ⌘K toggle still only changes that one device, the way it always has. But if you haven't set an account-wide default yet, a small prompt now offers to use it on all your devices. Take it and the choice syncs everywhere (and that device goes back to following your account default); ignore it and nothing changes. It stops asking once you've made an account-level choice, only ever appears a few times, and has a "Don't show again" if you'd rather it stay quiet.

### Fixes
- New workspaces provision more reliably. If a momentary database collision hit while your workspace was being created, signup could return a workspace that wasn't actually set up yet (its first page load failed). Provisioning now retries through that brief contention, so the workspace lands ready.

## 2026-07-05

### Features
- Knowledge Base entries can now hold an image: upload a screenshot (e.g. a scanner's config barcode, which must be *scanned from the image*, not regenerated) or any visual reference. It shows as a thumbnail in the list and full-size in Quick Access, so a pinned entry's barcode is scannable at a glance.
- New Knowledge Base module: keep rich Markdown entries (notes, references, SOPs, prompts, papers) in one place. Each entry has a Markdown body (with the Write/Preview editor), a category, an optional code, and a pin for quick access; search + filter by category. Enable it from Configuration.
- The 3D Printers page (and every Machines instance) now has saved views as chips: one click to switch perspective instead of re-checking a filter. Pick the shipped "Printer fleet by state" to group your printers by state, or hit Save view to name your own (which states to include + optional grouping) and pin it as a reusable tab. Your saved filter becomes a first-class chip, the same way Inventory and Projects already work.
- New QR field renderer: any text field (and a Knowledge Base entry's code) can render as a scannable QR of its value. Great for *owned* codes: a UPC, a location or asset tag, a URL. Pinned entries with a code now show a scannable QR in Quick Access.
- New Quick Access drawer: a floating button (shown when the Knowledge Base is enabled) opens a slide-over of your *pinned* entries, so a reference, code, or SOP you check often is one tap away from any page. Pin an entry in the Knowledge Base to add it here.
- Fields can now be Rich text: a Markdown editor with a Write/Preview split and a formatting toolbar (bold, italic, headings, lists, code, links). Ideal for long descriptions, notes, SOPs, and the knowledge base. Renders as formatted Markdown on detail pages and a clean one-line summary in tables.

### Improvements
- When Cobblr can't be reached (a deploy blip, a dropped connection), you now see one calm "Can't reach Cobblr, reconnecting…" bar at the top instead of scattered raw errors. It clears itself automatically the moment the service is back.
- Ask Cobb answers more out of the box. Even with no AI connected and nothing trained, Cobb now fields ~20 common questions: "what is Cobblr", find & search, add/edit/delete something, enable a module, custom fields, invite people, settings, export & backup, labels & QR, mobile, account & password, and feedback, on top of the originals. It's the untrained baseline every workspace gets; you can still tweak or add to it under Configuration → Assistant.
- DMing the Cobblr Discord bot a bug or idea now just gets a 👀: the "this is feedback & support" heads-up shows only once, on your first DM, instead of on every report.
- Picking a location no longer means scrolling one endless indented dropdown. The scan inbox's location pickers (New-scans target, bulk "Set location", and the confirm form) now use a drill-down: rooms open into their contents with a breadcrumb (Kitchen ▸ Rack 1 ▸ Shelf 2), plus a search box to jump straight to any bin, and "New location…" inline. Tap a name to pick it, tap ▸ to open it.
- Scan routing suggestions are less noisy and more consistent. The matchmaker now asks the model for one primary table plus at most one secondary (only when an item actually belongs in two), and never to list the same table twice, instead of padding "up to 3" with a duplicate or a marginal table. So a batch of the same kind of thing (a shelf of books) routes the same way, instead of some getting a stray "Collections" and others a duplicate chip.
- Self-hosters can now control exactly which third-party product catalogs barcode scanning contacts: a master switch (`COBBLR_SCAN_EXTERNAL_LOOKUPS`), independent per-provider toggles (upcitemdb, Open Facts, DuckDuckGo, go-upc), and API-key fields where a provider offers one. go-upc uses its official API when you supply a key; its web scraper is now off by default.
- Photographed books now get their ISBN backfilled from Open Library. An ISBN is almost never printed where a camera can read it, so when a book's match has no ISBN, Cobblr looks it up by title + author: preferring the edition whose publisher matches the book in hand (e.g. the Scholastic paperback, not a random hardcover). It only fills a blank ISBN (never overrides one the identify already found) and leaves it empty when there's no confident match rather than inventing one.
- Every "take a photo of this" spot in the scanner now uses the same in-app capture drawer (the scan result card's *Nice photo* and *Not it) photograph it*, plus the inbox item's *Retake for catalog* and *Add photo*. None of them launches the phone's native camera anymore: on the live scanner they reuse the running viewfinder, and elsewhere the drawer opens its own camera (with a photo-picker fallback when there's no camera). One consistent shutter → Retake / Use this flow everywhere.
- Scan cards now show what a match will fill, right on the collapsed card: the actual field values as mini chips (e.g. `genre Fiction · author Laura Ingalls Wilder · publisher Scholastic`) instead of a meaningless "fills 4". No need to open the item to see what's going into the table.
- Taking a "Nice photo" in the scanner no longer launches the phone's native camera on top of the one already running. It now opens an in-app capture drawer off the live viewfinder: frame it, hit the shutter, then Retake or Use this: and the catalog photo swaps in place with a clear "updated" confirmation instead of a fire-and-forget toast. (Desktop, with no live camera, still uses the file picker.)
- Scanning a whole series now routes uniformly. On top of the tightened matchmaker prompt, a deterministic pass aligns the *secondary* table suggestions across same-series items in a session: a shelf of Little House books either all offer a given secondary table (e.g. "Collections") or none do. No more the AI offering it on some books and not others. It only ever removes a stray secondary, never fabricates one, and never touches the primary table.

Profile → Appearance shows both: Account default (Match device / Light / Dark) and This device only (Use default / Light / Dark), so you can, say, keep a light default everywhere but force one shared display dark. Precedence is *this device → account default → the device's OS*, and the quick theme toggle in the header now sets this device only (toggling back toward your account default releases the lock so the device syncs again). The device override persists locally across reloads; it's never written to your account.

### Fixes
- Appearance is now an explicit setting in Profile → Appearance: Match device · Light · Dark. Before, the only way to change theme was a session toggle (an icon that's hidden on branded workspaces), and it only saved a preference if you happened to hit it, so if you never did, each device just followed its own OS and two devices could disagree ("dark on my Mac, light on my phone"). Now picking Dark (or Light) makes that choice explicit and it follows your account to every device, overriding each device's OS; Match device is a deliberate, saved choice that follows each device's OS live. - Stuck print jobs can be re-pointed at a printer: no delete-and-recreate. When a job's target matches no printer (or several), it lands in *awaiting assignment* instead of guessing. Now each such job in the queue has a Pick printer button: choose one from a searchable list and it's sent straight there, reusing the file Cobblr already uploaded, so there's no re-upload and no rebuilding the job from scratch. Picking a printer that isn't on that connection is refused, so a stray pick can't misroute the print.
- See your printer's webcam from anywhere: opt-in snapshot relay (off by default). A printer's camera is usually a LAN-only address, so it only shows in the Fleet when you're on the same network. Turn on Relay snapshots to cloud (the camera control on a Fleet card) and the edge agent pushes a frame every few seconds up to Cobblr, which serves the latest back, so you get a near-live thumbnail of the printer from your phone, anywhere, without exposing your camera or streaming full video. It's off until you switch it on (it uses upload bandwidth), and the tunnel itself still carries only stats, not video. This is the deliberate, cheap middle ground for remote viewing. Paired with the OctoPrint plugin's matching "relay snapshots" toggle.
- Migrating off FDM Monster? Digital Fabrication now has an Import farm button. Point it at your FDM Monster and bring its printers in two ways, your choice: connect to each printer directly: Cobblr recreates every printer as its own connection of the *matching* type (OctoPrint, Klipper/Moonraker, PrusaLink, FluidNC: per printer, not all-OctoPrint), installs the drivers it needs, pools them, and drops FDM Monster from the path; or keep FDM Monster and mirror its printers into a Cobblr pool. Either way the Fleet reads the imported machines as one farm.
- Upload a receipt and Cobblr reads it for you. On the Scan page, hit Receipt, pick a receipt PDF or a photo of one, and AI pulls out the vendor, date, and every line item. Each lands in your scan inbox as its own row, ready to confirm into a part the same way a barcode or photo scan does. No more typing a receipt in by hand.
- Spoolman users: Cobblr now talks to it. Connect your Spoolman (Inventory → Spoolman) and Sync: each spool comes in as an item with its measured remaining weight, and Cobblr treats Spoolman as the source of truth: it mirrors the number and won't deduct that spool itself (so a print counted by Moonraker → Spoolman isn't double-counted). Don't use Spoolman? Nothing changes. Cobblr's own consumption ledger keeps tracking. The right tracker for your setup, automatically.

### Fixes
- The Fleet view stays fast and gentle on a big farm. The live floor used to ask every connected machine manager for its printer list on every refresh, all at once: fine for a couple of printers, rough at fifty, where one slow manager could stall the whole view. Now each connection's printer list is briefly cached (a rapid refresh or a second screen reuses it), each fetch is time-boxed (a slow or unreachable manager falls back to its last-known state instead of freezing everyone), and the fetches run in bounded batches rather than a fifty-wide burst. Your in-flight jobs, links, and bed-clear flags are still read live every refresh, only the printer *state* can be a few seconds old.
- Print jobs honour printer tags on every farm, and won't land on the wrong machine. A job targeted at a tag (e.g. your "PLA" group) now resolves to a real printer inside Cobblr before it's sent, so it works the same on every connected farm, including FDM Monster's classic submit, which used to ignore the tag and let the farm route the file wherever it liked. And if a job points at a printer that isn't on its connection (a stale or mixed-up id), the send is refused with a clear error and the job is left in the queue to re-target, instead of silently printing on whatever machine happened to share that id.
